# Facing Conflict

## Definition
The ability of Mediators to confront interpersonal tensions or disagreements directly, rather than avoiding them or withdrawing, especially with personality types that favor direct, rational debate.

## Evidence
- *Source: [Hypersensitive to Criticism -> Facing Conflict](../sources/hypersensitive-criticism-to-facing-conflict.md)* introduces the necessity of confronting conflict despite the discomfort it causes for Mediators.
- In *[Caring or Naïve? — Second Opinions](../sources/caring-or-naive-to-second-opinions.md)*, the “Direct Communication” section provides a roadmap for engaging Analysts:
  - Approach with genuine curiosity and a desire to understand the disconnect.
  - Use direct yet sincere phrasing (e.g., “How can we work better together?”).
  - Ask thoughtful follow-up questions; Analysts appreciate deep, analytical inquiry.
  - Be honest and support opinions with facts or theories when reciprocating.
  The guide notes that avoiding conflict with Analysts is rarely productive; instead, Mediators should be direct and authentic.

## Open questions
- In what situations might direct communication backfire for Mediators, and how can they gauge the right tone?
- How can Mediators build confidence to initiate difficult conversations when they fear emotional fallout?
