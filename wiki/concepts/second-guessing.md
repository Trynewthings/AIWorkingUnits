# Second-Guessing

## Definition
Second‑guessing is a cognitive trap where Mediators overthink decisions and indulge in endless “what‑if” scenarios, often driven by low self‑confidence in emotional or ambiguous situations. It leads to hesitation, unfinished tasks, and a failure to act on ideas, ultimately reinforcing self‑doubt and withdrawal from collaboration.

## Evidence
- When Mediators let worries outweigh self‑confidence, they “overthink everything and indulging in endless *what‑ifs*” ([Slow It Down → Second‑Guessing](../sources/slow-it-down-to-second-guessing.md)).
- Explorers’ fast‑paced action can make Mediators feel labeled as “wet blankets,” worsening second‑guessing.
- Second‑guessing compromises the potential synergy between Mediators and Explorers, as Mediators retreat to escape judgment.

## Open questions
- How can Mediators distinguish between productive caution and destructive second‑guessing?
- What interventions (e.g., mindfulness, structured decision frameworks) reduce second‑guessing in the workplace?
