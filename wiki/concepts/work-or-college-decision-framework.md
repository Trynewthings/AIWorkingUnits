# Work or College Decision Framework

## Definition
A decision framework presented in the *Mediator Guide to Careers* specifically for Mediators facing the post-high-school choice between entering the workforce directly or pursuing higher education. Unlike other personality types who may default to college due to social expectation, Mediators must align this decision with their personal values and sense of meaning — otherwise they risk dissatisfaction and passive living.

**Key principles:**
- College is only worthwhile for Mediators if it aligns with their unique values; external pressure from family or authority figures leads to poor outcomes.
- Work right out of high school also requires a sense of purpose — "marking time" is equally unfulfilling.
- Gap years and alternative lifestyles (e.g., sustainable farming, world travel) are legitimate options for Mediators.
- The decision must combine both rationality (financial reality, time investment) and instinct.

**Self-assessment questions recommended by the guide:**
- When do you feel engaged and enthusiastic, work- or academic-wise?
- When have you overcome a difficult challenge successfully?
- When have you achieved peak performance? What were you doing? What principles did you follow?
- Bearing in mind current challenges, how can you build on what you know works?

## Evidence
- Covered in the "Work or College?" subsection of the Academic Path.
- Balanced decision-making: practicality + values alignment.
- Unbalanced patterns: appealing only to instinct (over-relying on intuition/feeling); selecting for others (people-pleasing).
- Rebalancing: assertively discover true preference; separate own desires from others' expectations.
