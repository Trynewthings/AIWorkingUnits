# Reassessing Praise and Criticism

## Definition
Reassessing praise and criticism is a cognitive reframing strategy that helps Mediators depersonalize feedback. Instead of reacting emotionally, they analyze feedback as information about the person giving it, not an objective judgment of their worth. This reduces hypersensitivity and allows Mediators to incorporate constructive insights while discarding unhelpful opinions.

## Evidence
- The Mediator Guide suggests using a formula: “My managers believe [X]. This teaches me that they would prefer [Y].” (see [Source: Hypersensitive to Criticism -> Facing Conflict](../sources/hypersensitive-criticism-to-facing-conflict.md)).
- It advises Mediators to decide whether to honor the corollary preference, and if the opinion seems out of line, to discuss it directly to clear miscommunications.

## Open questions
- How can this formula be applied to non-hierarchical relationships (e.g., peers, clients)?
- To what extent does this strategy risk rationalizing away legitimate, if painful, feedback?
- Can repeated use of this reframing build resilience over time?
