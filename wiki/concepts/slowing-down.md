# Slowing Down

## Definition
Slowing down is a deliberate workplace strategy for Mediators to temper their idealistic impulses by systematically assessing each stage of a project. It involves taking time to define benchmarks, anticipate pitfalls, and enlist colleagues with different thinking styles to flesh out actionable plans, without sacrificing optimism.

## Evidence
- Mediators are advised to “take a moment to assess it” by answering questions about people‑hours, benchmarks, contributors, and trade‑offs ([Slow It Down → Second‑Guessing](../sources/slow-it-down-to-second-guessing.md)).
- Enlisting Sentinels and Explorers helps translate big ideas into realistic steps.
- The approach balances Mediators’ intuition with practical execution, preventing derailment or distraction.

## Open questions
- How can Mediators maintain their spontaneous creativity while adopting structured slowness?
- Does slowing down risk losing the “big picture” inspiration that drives Mediators’ work?
