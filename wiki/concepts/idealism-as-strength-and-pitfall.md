# Idealism as Strength and Pitfall

## Definition
Idealism in the INFP/Mediator context is the innate tendency to envision a better future and to strive for high‑minded goals, often rooted in personal values. As a strength, it fuels creativity, passion, and innovation. However, unchecked idealism can become a pitfall when it leads to unrealistic expectations, frustration with practical constraints, and overlooking necessary details.

## Evidence
- Early sources note that Mediators’ idealism can make them vulnerable to disappointment when reality falls short (e.g., [Tempered Idealism to Restless Hearts](../sources/tempered-idealism-to-restless-hearts.md)).
- The [Creative Arts to Helpful Accountability](../sources/creative-arts-to-helpful-accountability.md) source warns that idealism may cause Mediators to take on too much, leading to burnout.
- In [Slow It Down → Second‑Guessing](../sources/slow-it-down-to-second-guessing.md), the advice to slow down and assess projects step‑by‑step shows that Mediators’ idealistic leaps can be tempered without killing their spirit.
- The same source highlights that Sentinels may view Mediators’ constant search for improvement as “messing with success,” creating workplace conflict.

## Open questions
- Where is the line between necessary idealistic drive and counterproductive perfectionism?
- How can Mediators maintain optimism while internalizing practical feedback from non‑Diplomat types?
