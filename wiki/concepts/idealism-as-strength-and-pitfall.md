# Idealism as Strength and Pitfall

## Definition
Idealism is described as perhaps the Mediator's defining trait — a persistent, lifelong orientation toward meaning, beauty, human potential, and a better world. It fuels their motivation, creativity, and sense of purpose. However, the same idealism can become a liability when it:
- Creates an overwhelming gap between vision and concrete action ("Step A to Step Z" problem)
- Leads to over-excitement about new subjects followed by paralysis before starting
- Causes disappointment when the effort required to master something becomes apparent
- Results in indulgent learning (only pursuing what inspires, neglecting what is needed)

The book describes Mediators as "masters of correlation" (seeing how everything fits together) but not always equally skilled at "causation" (understanding where things start and end).

## Evidence
- Introduced in the Introduction as the source of Mediators' vision and ambition.
- Revisited in "Lifelong Learning" as "Only Idealism" — an unbalanced pattern where excitement precedes commitment but follow-through is absent.
- Rebalancing strategy: temper idealism with research before committing; develop a creative plan with small, concrete steps; visualize happiness on your own terms (not society's).
- The guide recommends: "ruminate less and implement more."
