# People-Pleasing Pitfall

## Definition
A recurring behavioral pattern for Mediators in which deference to the expectations, opinions, or wishes of authority figures (parents, teachers, mentors) or loved ones overrides the Mediator's own values and authentic preferences. The guide describes this as a path toward "passive living" — allowing others to make the majority of life's choices, even choices the Mediator doesn't personally endorse.

This pitfall is distinct from healthy consideration of others' input. It becomes problematic when:
- College or career choices are made primarily to satisfy family expectations
- The Mediator's own desires are suppressed or never clearly articulated
- Acquiescing to others becomes the default because the Mediator's own desires feel "too overwhelmingly complex" to sort out

**Rebalancing strategy:** Mediators must assertively discover their true preferences and separate them from others' expectations. They cannot be responsible for others' reactions to their authentic choices. Faithfulness to personal values is described as "the highest form of authenticity."

## Evidence
- Introduced in the "Selecting for Others" subsection of "Work or College?"
- Reinforced in the Introduction's discussion of sensitivity and vulnerability to criticism.
- The guide notes that Mediators are "always conscientious of societal expectations" and may pick routes deemed "proper" or "acceptable" by authority figures.

## Open questions
- How does the people-pleasing pitfall interact with Mediators' genuine care for others and desire for harmony — where is the line between authentic consideration and self-suppression?
- Does this pitfall diminish with age and self-knowledge, or does it persist throughout life?
