# Concrete Communication for Mediators

## Definition

Concrete communication is the practice of transforming broad, idealistic, or abstract requests into specific, immediately solvable problems, especially when working with action-oriented colleagues like Explorers. It involves clearly stating what needs to be done, using colloquial language, ending with a call to action, and avoiding lengthy philosophical justifications.

## Evidence

- **Source: [Calls to Action – So… What’s Next?](../sources/calls-to-action-to-whats-next.md)**: Mediators are advised to ask, “Can you help me find the bug on this web page?” instead of vague requests like “Can you improve our company’s image?” Lengthy explanations are discouraged because they disengage Explorer types. Clear, present-moment problems capture their interest and lead to effective collaboration.

## Open questions

- How can Mediators balance their natural inclination to explore meaning while employing concrete communication in hierarchical or cross-functional teams?
- Does over-reliance on concrete framing mask deeper value misalignments that Mediators might need to address?
