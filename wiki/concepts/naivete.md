# Naïveté

## Definition
A label often applied to Mediators (INFPs) and other Feeling-Perceiving types when their idealism and trust in people’s good intentions are perceived as unrealistic or overly optimistic by more skeptical or analytical colleagues. In the workplace, this can lead to their contributions being dismissed.

## Evidence
- *Source: [Mediator Guide to Careers](../sources/mediator-guide-to-careers.md)* (initial chapters) notes that Mediators’ natural idealism can be seen as naïveté by those who prioritize pragmatism.
- In *[Caring or Naïve? — Second Opinions](../sources/caring-or-naive-to-second-opinions.md)*, Mediators are explicitly called “naïve” or “soft” by Analyst colleagues when they advocate for kindness over logic. The text states: “When Mediators voice their objections, they might find themselves labeled ‘naïve’ or ‘soft.’” The source advises Mediators to reframe such responses as differences in communication style rather than personal attacks.

## Open questions
- Is naïveté simply a pejorative for a principled, relationship-centered approach, or are there contexts where it truly undermines Mediators’ effectiveness?
- How can Mediators communicate their values in ways that pragmatic colleagues find more credible?
