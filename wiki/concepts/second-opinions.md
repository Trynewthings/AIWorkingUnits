# Second Opinions

## Definition
The deliberate practice of seeking external feedback and alternative perspectives from colleagues with diverse strengths, particularly to counteract the insular idealism that can arise when Mediators and Diplomats share too homogeneous a view. It involves structured steps to request, receive, and integrate advice without mistaking opinion for directive.

## Evidence
- In *[Caring or Naïve? — Second Opinions](../sources/caring-or-naive-to-second-opinions.md)*, the guide outlines a comprehensive approach:
  - Create a list of colleagues and note their strengths (troubleshooting, financial analysis, workplace politics).
  - Approach individuals one-on-one, clarifying you’d like their opinion.
  - Ask specific, open-ended questions (e.g., “Does this seem feasible to you?”).
  - Establish expectations that it’s an opinion, not a directive.
  - Take notes during the conversation and circle action items afterward.
  - Follow up with thanks and updates on implementation.
  - Remind yourself that people generally feel flattered when asked for opinions.
