# Slow It Down → Second-Guessing

## Summary
This source provides Mediators with strategies to slow down and craft their idealistic goals carefully by breaking them into actionable steps and leveraging colleagues with different thinking styles. It then explores workplace dynamics with Sentinel and Explorer types, highlighting both complementarities and tensions, such as navigating rules vs. flexibility, and the pitfalls of second-guessing and overthinking.

## Key claims
- Mediators should slow down and assess each stage of a project, not assume success.
- Collaborating with Sentinels creates a human-centric synergy: Mediators offer high-level vision, Sentinels handle practicalities.
- Mediators can mitigate insecurities by adopting project management frameworks (e.g., SMART goals) to prove their ideas to Sentinels.
- Explorers help Mediators stay present and motivated, but clashes arise over values vs. utility.
- Second‑guessing and overthinking undermine Mediators’ confidence and collaboration, leading to withdrawal and lost potential.

## Concepts touched
- Idealism and its balancing with practicality
- Slowing down and step‑wise planning

- Second‑guessing and overthinking
- Values vs. utility in the workplace
- Perfectionism and fear of criticism

## Quotes
> “Idealistic goals often benefit the workplace, but they must be crafted one piece at a time with focus and care, not just hope.”
> “Without sacrificing their trademark optimism, Mediators and Diplomats can support each other to translate their big ideas into action steps and timelines.”
> “If their worries outweigh their self-confidence, Mediators fall into the trap of overthinking everything and indulging in endless what-ifs.”
