# Source: Mediator Guide to Careers

## Summary
A comprehensive career and academic guide written specifically for the INFP ("Mediator") personality type. The book covers the full arc from high school learning habits through college decision-making, lifelong learning, and professional development. It frames Mediator strengths (intuition, empathy, creativity, idealism) and weaknesses (avoidance of detail, fixed mindset, people-pleasing) as actionable opportunities, offering practical exercises throughout.

## Key claims
- Mediators are intuitive learners who absorb patterns subconsciously and work backwards from conclusions to details.
- Mediators thrive in humanities-oriented subjects and careers with a human/creative element; they struggle with rote memorization and purely technical work.
- A "fixed mindset" (Carol Dweck's framework) is a particular risk for Mediators who were told they were gifted early in life.
- The most important career question for Mediators is not "What do I want to be?" but "Who do I want to be?"
- Mediators need creative roles and a sense of purpose; routine, impersonal, or data-heavy work leaves them restless and unfulfilled.
- Introversion means prolonged social contact at work can be exhausting, even if the work is fulfilling.
- Balanced decision-making requires combining rationality with instinct, especially for high-stakes choices like college.
- People-pleasing and deference to authority figures is a recurring pitfall that leads to passive living.
- Lifelong learning is natural for Mediators but can be undermined by over-idealism and failure to follow through on implementation.
