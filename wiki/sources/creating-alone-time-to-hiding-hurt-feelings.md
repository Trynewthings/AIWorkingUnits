# Source: Creating Alone Time to Hiding Hurt Feelings

## Summary
This source from the Professional Development part of the Mediator Guide to Careers covers strategies for Mediators to thrive in the workplace, including carving out alone time to avoid burnout, their egalitarian and talent-cultivating leadership style, and the challenges of conflict avoidance, perfectionism, and sensitivity to criticism. It also explores collaboration with Analyst personality types, noting both complementary strengths and potential pitfalls such as hiding hurt feelings.

## Key claims
- Mediators need regular alone time to restore energy; creative strategies (headphones, solitary lunches, independent tasks) can provide it even in open-plan offices.
- As managers, Mediators create democratic, non-hierarchical teams, encouraging innovation and giving junior staff credit.
- Their conflict aversion and desire to see the best in others can cause leniency toward poor performance, damaging team morale.
- Leadership pressure can trigger perfectionism, procrastination, and feelings of helplessness; setting realistic short-term goals and celebrating progress is essential.
- Positive reinforcement (self-fulfilling prophecy) is a more effective management tool for Mediators than criticism.
- Mediators and Analysts share big-picture, pattern-recognition thinking and can innovate together, but Mediators’ sensitivity may interpret Analysts’ bluntness as personal attacks, leading to disengagement and hidden hurt.
