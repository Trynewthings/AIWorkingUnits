# Caring or Naïve? — Second Opinions

## Summary
This section from the *Mediator Guide to Careers* addresses workplace dynamics between Mediators (INFPs) and two other personality groups: Analysts (NT types) and Diplomats (NF types). It offers practical advice for Mediators to navigate feeling dismissed by Analysts, not taking brusque feedback personally, and engaging in direct communication. For Diplomat colleagues, it highlights the shared idealism but warns against cliquishness and inaction, advocating for actively seeking second opinions to balance perspectives.

## Key claims
- Analysts often prioritize logic over feelings, causing Mediators to be labeled “naïve” or “soft” when they object.
- Mediators’ hurt feelings from Analysts’ bluntness are usually not personal but a result of efficiency-driven communication.
- Adopting alternative explanations and reframing expectations can reduce sensitivity.
- Direct, candid, and curious questioning is the most effective way for Mediators to communicate with Analysts.
- Mediators and Diplomats thrive on shared visionary language but risk gridlock if they avoid tough decisions or isolate themselves.
- Actively seeking second opinions from colleagues with different strengths counteracts idealism and cliquishness.
- Step-by-step strategies for soliciting help include making a colleague list, asking specific questions, and taking notes.

## Entities mentioned
- Analysts (INTJ, INTP, ENTJ, ENTP)
- Diplomats (INFJ, ENFJ, INFP, ENFP)
- Mediators (INFP)
