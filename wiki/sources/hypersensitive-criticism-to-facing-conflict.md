# Source: Hypersensitive to Criticism -> Facing Conflict

## Summary
This source covers pages 38-43 from the Mediator Guide to Careers (Professional Development part). It addresses Mediators' hypersensitivity to criticism, the resulting pitfalls like perfectionism and withdrawal, and techniques to reframe feedback. It then discusses disillusionment when workplace values clash, strategies to quash helplessness, and the Mediator's role as a colleague—highlighting goodwill, empathy, naïveté, and the need for solitude. Finally, it provides a framework for deciding when and how to face workplace conflict.

## Key claims
- Mediators are highly sensitive to criticism, leading them to amplify self-criticism or shut down.
- Disillusionment arises when the workplace fails to uphold moral or idealistic principles, sapping motivation.
- Feedback can be depersonalized by interpreting it as information about the giver’s preferences, using a simple formula.
- Concrete action, even small steps, can combat helplessness and restore a sense of agency.
- As colleagues, Mediators contribute empathy and goodwill that boosts team productivity, but their positivity may be read as naïveté and they may avoid necessary conflict.
- Solitude is essential for Mediators to recharge and generate ideas; lack of it leads to negativity and underperformance.
- In conflict, Mediators should first assess whether the situation warrants their involvement before jumping in.
