# Facts Thrive with Context to Appealing Only to Instinct

## Summary
This source compiles later sections of the Academic Path part from *Mediator Guide to Careers*. It addresses how Mediators can make factual learning engaging through mnemonic devices and storytelling, the pitfalls of a fixed mindset, and provides a detailed framework for the work-or-college decision. The final section warns against over-reliance on intuition without grounding in facts.

## Key claims
- Mediators learn facts best when couched in meaningful, human contexts or stories (e.g., mnemonic devices blending mythology and science).
- In high school, Mediators may excel in humanities but struggle with rote memorization and mundane subjects; they can reframe such subjects as exercises in personal growth.
- A fixed mindset can limit Mediators who coast on early natural talent; adopting a growth mindset helps them embrace failure as an opportunity.
- The decision to pursue college, work, or alternative paths must align deeply with the Mediator’s need for meaning and authenticity.
- Mediators’ Prospecting and Intuitive traits can lead to scattered interests and over-reliance on instinct; balancing intuition with objective facts is crucial for sound decisions.
