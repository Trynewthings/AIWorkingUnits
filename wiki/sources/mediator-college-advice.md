# Mediator College Advice: Selecting for Others to Humbly Genuine

## Summary
This source provides advice for Mediators (INFPs) on making authentic college decisions and navigating academic life. It warns against people-pleasing, offers a self-discovery exercise, highlights the importance of humanistic relevance in course selection, and addresses pitfalls like neglecting fundamentals and rushing to conclusions intuitively. It introduces the "Yes, and..." improv technique as a way to stay genuine while engaging with challenging material.

## Key claims
- Mediators may let family or mentors sway their life choices, risking passive living.
- Separating personal desires from others' expectations is critical for authentic decision-making.
- A self-assessment exercise helps identify what gives meaning and when peak performance occurs.
- College can be a haven for Mediators if they choose courses with a human element.
- Unappealing prerequisites can be reframed as self-discipline training.
- Intuitive leaps can cause missed steps and stubbornness; balancing with analytical process is needed.
- The improv mindset ("Yes, and...") helps accept premises while adding personal insight, resolving authenticity-harmony conflicts.
