# Source: Solidifying the Abstract

## Summary
This section from the Academic Path chapter discusses how Mediators can transform their abstract thoughts into concrete communication. It emphasizes the importance of verbalizing or writing out ideas to solidify them, and offers a practical process for distilling vague notions into clear points.

## Key claims
- Mediators often have unconventional or fantastical ideas that they may hesitate to share.
- Writing and speaking about abstract thoughts helps turn them into measurable, understandable concepts.
- The process of observation and distillation—writing, identifying patterns, describing observations, and refining—can clarify thinking.

## Quotes
- "Communication is an important part of Meditators’ learning style, and they solidify their abstract notions when verbalizing their thoughts or writing them out."
- "It’s like describing the shape of smoke. Before they can measure the ideas in their head, they must first contain them."
- "Read over some previously written work or write down new thoughts for about 30 minutes... After reading or writing, go over the work again and pick out the most prevalent/interesting/recurring patterns."
